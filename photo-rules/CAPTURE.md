# Taking reference photos

Online collections are full of flowers and short of whole plants. Photos
taken to this protocol fill that gap and are worth more than dozens of
random ones.

## Before you start

- **Pick plants that grew in the open** (a lone tree in a field, a shrub at
  a clearing's edge), unless you are documenting the forest form. Note which.
- **Avoid** pruned, staked, sheared or obviously planted plants, unless the
  species is only cultivated, and say so.
- Note the date, place (coordinates if you can), and anything you know about
  the plant's age.

## The shots, in order

1. **Whole plant, side (V1).** Stand back about twice the plant's height,
   camera at eye height, level (not tilted up). Base and top in frame, with
   a little sky above. Put a person or a pole of known length beside the
   trunk for scale. Take it from two directions at right angles.
2. **Whole plant leafless (V2).** For deciduous trees and shrubs, the same
   shot in winter, from the same spots.
3. **From above (V3).** For herbs, rosettes and cacti: from 45 degrees down
   and straight down, with a ruler or your hand in frame.
4. **A branch (V4).** A typical shoot from the middle of the crown, 0.5-1 m
   of it, from the side, against the sky or a plain background, leaves in
   place.
5. **Leaves (V5).** Three typical mature leaves laid flat on a plain sheet
   beside a ruler, face up and one face down. Don't use the biggest or the
   odd one.
6. **Flowers (V6, V7).** One flower straight on, one from the side; the whole
   cluster from the side; a ruler in one of them.
7. **Fruit or cones (V8).** In place on the branch, then one beside a ruler.
8. **Bark or stem (V8).** At chest height, straight on, about 0.5 m across,
   in shade or overcast light.

## Light

- Overcast or open shade is best for colour and texture; direct sun is best
  for gloss. Say which.
- No flash for whole plants. A grey card or a white sheet in one frame helps
  colour.

## File the photos

Name them `<species>-<place>-<date>-<view>-<n>.jpg`, and keep a list with
the date, place, setting (open-grown, forest, garden), age if known, and the
licence you give them.
